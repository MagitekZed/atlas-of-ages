1.12

Magical Sicknesses

Chunk 1228 OctoberMythic worlds only

Three plagues only magic can cause, and a fourth thing that can end an age. Historical and Legendary worlds play out exactly as they did before.

  • NewMythicThe grave-plague. A realm that knows Necromancy, led by a merciless ruler with no healing faith, may lay one on an enemy city when its war turns desperate. It kills hard in that city and little beyond it, and the dead rise bound to the caster for three years. The world dreads it, and leagues form against whoever cast it. Very rarely, in a crowded late world, one spreads far. In testing one took 7 % of a world, and that is staying in: a very small chance of apocalypse.
  • NewMythicMana-burn. Draw a town’s magic hard against its land and mana-burn breaks out among its mages and its elves. Men, halflings and orcs barely catch it, and no custom or quarantine keeps it out. It burns the land’s mana for a generation, and a rare art can die with its keepers.
  • NewMythicThe Wasting. A ruler wounded by a curse, a beast of legend, a broken oath or a razed holy place bears a wound that will not close, and the harvests fail around their seat. The people blame the gods, a neighbour, the mages or a minority until the truth comes out, often by confession. About ten years on the reign ends by deposition, illness, a cure, abdication or murder.
  • NewMythicThe Scourge. A host risen from land thick with the dead and with magic can grow until it is given a name. It usually breaks free of its maker, blights the land, and the realms it threatens hunt it together. Starve it and it sleeps in a barrow, to wake when fresh foes come near. Armies grind it down, or a company of heroes destroys it, often after decades.
  • NewMythicDarkness, and the Age of the Dead. A generation after a Scourge is named, darkness falls on its region. A Scourge that carries its war beyond that region brings on a new world age: the Age of the Dead.
  • BalanceMythicA Scourge fights like a war, not a beast. It gives battle once a month at most, each defeat costs it 5 to 25 % of its strength, and its dead carry the weapons of the foes they fell to. Each realm sends a share of a common hunt rather than its whole army, and no company of heroes dares it in its first five years. Named Scourges now last 18 to 88 years. Before this change they died within a year.
  • BalanceMythicScourges are a little less rare: about one Mythic world in twelve now raises one. Expect about two Wastings and two outbreaks of mana-burn in a Mythic world’s thousand years.
  • ChangedMythicSoldiers a Scourge kills now count toward its darkness, so a Scourge at war darkens its region the way one feeding on towns does.
  • ChangedMythicRestless sleepers keep one barrow. A Scourge that sleeps again soon goes back to its old barrow, and its later sleeps and wakings are told in passing. One test Scourge went from 72 Chronicle lines and 36 barrows to 5 lines and 1 barrow.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.11:
    PopulationRealmsTownsWarsPlague dead
    Mythic+4 %+3 %+3 %0 %−9 %
     Historical and Legendary worlds are identical to 1.11.
1.11

Health and Sickness

Chunk 1128 October

Every people falls sick in its own way, cities breed disease, and armies carry fever with them.

  • NewPeoples and sickness. Each people has its own frailty, a hardiness left by old plagues and great cities, and a memory of which sicknesses it has met. A plague brought by strangers it has never met can devastate it. A plague’s account now names whom it took and why.
  • NewCustoms of cleanliness. A people bathes, washes by rite, keeps its wells apart, keeps a clean hearth, does none of these, or fears water. A great plague can shut the bathhouses, and reformers and faiths change customs.
  • NewHealing faiths ease a plague’s deaths, and their nursing monks win converts. Penitent faiths send flagellants through the towns, and pilgrims carry a plague home from a holy city.
  • NewPhysicians and their theories. Realms climb from spirits to the humours, then to bad air or contagion, then to germs. Quarantines come with the newer theories. Bleeding under the humours makes plagues a little deadlier and shortens a sick ruler’s life.
  • NewHousing and sanitation. Crowded great cities grow slums that breed sickness. Towns build in timber, mud brick or stone according to their ground, and that sets their risk of fire.
  • NewFever country. Marshes and hot wet forest cost a town health and kill newcomers until they are seasoned. Settlers avoid fever ground, and a realm can drain a marsh as a great work. About one Historical town in ten stands on fever ground.
  • NewCamp sickness. Camp fever grows with an army’s months in the field, siege lines, hunger and summer heat. Some of the sick die and some desert. In the long wars of later ages it kills more soldiers than battle does, and very rarely a great host carries it home as a plague.
  • NewMythicEach race has its own customs and healing lore, and its own frailty: elves 0.4, lizardfolk 0.6, dwarves 0.7, halflings 0.9, humans 1, orcs 1.4. Lizardfolk bear fever country fully and elves half.
  • BalanceFever along hot rivers was dropped and fever ground made lighter after testing showed it held back every world’s leading realm.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.10:
    PopulationRealmsTownsWarsPlague dead
    Historical−2 %−1 %0 %+5 %+110 %
    Mythic+3 %−1 %+5 %+9 %−51 %
     Historical plague deaths doubled, mostly from first-contact plagues reaching peoples with no defence against them. The hardier Mythic races lost half as many.
  • SpeedThe new health system costs about 2 ms per simulated year.
1.10

Peoples and Memory

Chunk 827 October

Peoples love, hate, mix and forget, each at its own pace.

  • NewFeelings between peoples, from −100 to 100. They move with wars fought against or beside each other, razings, conquests, liberations, trade, royal matches and persecution, and drift back toward where they rest. Feelings shape realms’ opinions of each other, where migrants are welcome and how minorities are treated.
  • NewMythicOld hatreds. Elves and orcs start at −60, dwarves and orcs −55, halflings and orcs −35, humans and orcs −30, elves and dwarves −15, and lizardfolk −20 with everyone. A long alliance can heal a hatred, rarely, and long wars deepen one. A hatred of −50 or worse starts wars of enmity and makes razing likelier.
  • NewMythicHow long each remembers. Grudges and kindnesses fade at each people’s pace: elves for centuries, orcs within a generation, men over a few lifetimes. Historical peoples remember as men do.
  • NewCultural distance. How alike two realms are, from their peoples, the sounds of their tongues, their faiths and their customs. Close realms drift warmer and marry more, and distance slows assimilation.
  • NewPolicy toward minorities. Each minority is equal, tolerated, taxed, persecuted or expelled, decided by the ruler’s mercy, piety and greed, the ruling people’s feelings, hard times and the minority’s numbers. Persecution drives the young away and breeds pogroms in hard times, and an expelled people goes to its kin abroad. Most minorities are tolerated.
  • NewDiasporas. About half of Historical towns now hold a minority, carried by migrants, merchants, refugees and conquerors. A people that lives apart from its kin for about three centuries becomes a new people.
  • NewAssimilation. A small, close minority takes on its town’s ways in about three generations. Large, distant or persecuted ones hold on for centuries. Peoples can vanish into others, and a people that lost every realm may rise again in a revolt.
  • NewWaves of migration. Crowding, hunger, war and sieges can set part of a region moving into the towns of a calmer one nearby. Expect about five great migrations in a Historical world’s thousand years.

1.9

Sieges and Walls

Chunk 627 October

Every wall has a story now, and every siege has an ending.

  • NewWalls in detail. A walled town keeps a palisade, stone walls, a castle or a star fort, with its condition, towers, moat and citadel, and the ground it stands on: hill, crag, river, marsh or sea. Sieges, quakes and sacks wear walls down, and they are mended while someone pays. Walled towns lay in stores in wartime, and a conqueror sometimes tears down the walls of a town it fears.
  • NewMythicEach people builds its own walls: dwarven rune-walls that mend themselves, elven thorn-walls that never burn, orc stake-walls, lizardfolk water-forts and halfling hedge-banks.
  • NewSiegecraft. A siege eats the town’s food and then its stores, though a harbour is fed from the sea while the besieger has no fleet. Engines wear the walls to a breach, palisades burn, and guns break anything short of a star fort. Mines are dug where the ground allows. Each month the besieger may storm, the garrison may sally, and a gate may be betrayed or bought.
  • NewSurrender on terms. A starving town that surrenders is spared the sack.
  • NewGreat sieges. A siege of a year and a half or more is named (“the Great Siege of…”) and remembered on its ground.
  • BalanceSiege lengths spread out. Rich, well-fortified, well-stocked towns hold far longer, and poorly stocked ones fall fast. Towns now fall stormed 54 % of the time, surrendered 20 %, mined 12 % and betrayed 11 %. Towns captured rose 37 %.

    Mythic+1 %+8 %
1.8

Dynasties

Chunk 927 October

Royal houses get family trees, heirs and pretenders, and crowns worth stealing.

  • NewFamily trees. Ruling houses keep their families: parents, brothers and sisters, spouses from abroad, children born and lost, nephews, uncles, cousins and bastards. Every kept person has a record and every house a family tree, about 760 people in a world.
  • NewThe line of succession, several heirs deep and set by the realm’s law: sons before daughters until the later ages, then the eldest child. A child ruler’s regent is their mother or an uncle.
  • NewMythicEach race inherits its own way. Halflings crown the eldest child, orcs the strongest, and lizardfolk inherit through the mother.
  • NewPretenders. A succession crisis raises a named pretender from the tree: an uncle over a child king, a brother, a bastard. Rulers of low honour father bastards, and a ruler with no lawful child may make one lawful. A realm under divided inheritance splits only when there is a real second heir.
  • NewThe royal kinship web. Houses arrange matches abroad, about one every four years in a world. When a house dies out at home the next in blood abroad inherits, joining two crowns, unless the great refuse a foreigner and crown one of their own. A war over the cousin’s throne may follow. A restored realm crowns its exiled house.
  • NewNamed treasures. Crowns, swords of state, seals and sceptres, named blades for great captains, and the holy relics of each faith, each with a maker and a story. They are carried off from fallen capitals, lost in sacks, buried with heroes, found in ruins and given back at peace.
  • NewMythicTreasures of power. Runeblades, starglass, war-totems, egg-stones and heirloom books lend their holder a gift.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.7:
    RealmsWars
    Historical−2 %−20 %
    Mythic−18 %−27 %
  • SpeedFaster: Historical worlds run 0.9 % quicker and Mythic worlds 10 % quicker.

Who Holds Power

Chunk 727 October

Nobles, priests, guilds and courts all want something, and treasuries stop hoarding.

  • NewSocial estates. The great, the clergy, townsfolk, commoners and the unfree each hold a share of the people and the wealth, with a loyalty moved by taxes, hunger, war, the coin, faith, bondage and the court. Push one too far and it rises: peasant risings, noble revolts, townsmen’s charters, interdicts, the bound in revolt.
  • NewMythicEach people has its own estates: dwarven thanes and thralls, elven great houses with no unfree, lizardfolk brood-lords and egg-priests.
  • NewLaws. Every realm has four: inheritance (eldest, divided, elective or seniority), faith (one, tolerated or free), bondage (none, serfdom or slavery) and land (crown, nobles or freeholds). Rulers, estates and new ages reform them.
  • NewDivided inheritance. A realm of eight or more towns may split between two heirs, about once a century in a world. The sister realms start allied and keep their claims on each other.
  • NewCourt factions. Up to five per court (the army, the church, the old blood, the guilds, the reformers), each with a named leader, sway and a demand. A refused faction may stage a coup, an interdict or a revolution, or a ruthless ruler may purge it.
  • ChangedMoney drains in peacetime. The court takes 3 to 10 % of income and spends down savings above a reserve of three to five years. Misers, merchant states and dwarven holds keep more. Treasuries now hold months to a few years of income instead of decades.
  • NewRuin after revolt. A revolt, a civil war or a sacked capital leaves years of lost income behind it.
  • NewCorruption. Officials keep 5 to 25 % of the taxes, more in large, old and overstretched realms. A stern steward, reformers and purges cut it back.
  • NewDebased coin. A crown deep in arrears may clip its coin for a few months’ pay. The coin’s bad name costs income and the estates’ loyalty until it is restored.
  • ChangedTrades shape the estates. A town’s occupations from 1.1 now set its realm’s estates, and risings go by estate.
  • BalanceSplits made rarer after the first tests showed a split every 25 to 30 years in each world.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.6.1:

    Historical+31 %about the same

     The extra realms come from collapses, rebellions and civil wars where there used to be one great empire.

The Hand’s Powers, Tuned

27 Octoberafter an audit of every power

The new reports showed some powers hitting softer than they look. Ten fixes, and two more for the brush and the letters.

  • BalanceEarthquakes kill twice the share of a struck town. An M9.5 takes about 40 % of the town at its heart. Any undersea quake of 7.5 or more now raises a wave.
  • BalanceWaves drown twice as many, still capped at 60 % of a town. This covers the Tsunami power, undersea quakes, falling stars at sea and natural waves.
  • BalanceStorms raised by the hand strike up to three times harder at full fury. Over land that is neither desert nor frozen, Storm now raises a tempest that hits towns the way a cyclone does at landfall.
  • BalanceFloods are three times deadlier, the hand’s and natural ones alike.
  • ChangedForged alliances hold. An alliance the hand forges cannot lapse for twenty years. War between the two still ends it.
  • FixedRaise Island no longer drowns towns. Beside a settled shore a new island leaves a strait of sea, so it never dams a bay into a lake that fills over the town. A stretch of sea closed off during play stays sea, as a lagoon.
  • ChangedLower’s forecast now gives the people at risk and the severity, as Sink’s does.
  • BalanceA great quake’s fault lifts the land about 30 m at M9.5, down from about 170 m.
  • Fixed“What came of it” measures a power’s whole reach. Earthquakes, eruptions, falling stars, tsunamis, storms, lightning and locusts are now judged over the ring their forecast draws, not just the clicked tile.
  • NewThe hand’s fires reach towns. A Wildfire or a lightning blaze burning next to a town burns into it once: buildings, stores and a few percent of its people, more when the land is dry.
  • ChangedReports in plain words. Each moment of a report is a small table, set beside the whole world.
  • FixedA brush held while time runs counts as one act of the hand, not dozens.
  • FixedA war’s letter opens the war it tells of.
1.6

Seeing and the Hand

Chunk 1027 Octoberfrom playtest feedback

Watch your wars properly, and find out what your meddling actually did.

  • NewA war watch. A watched war’s chip shows each side’s leader and allies, towns taken and razed, the fallen, each side’s aim and the latest battle, and its dossier tells the same above the score. While a war is watched or selected the map outlines both sides, draws the front between them and rings every siege.
  • NewWhat came of it. Every act of the hand is measured before, right after, when its work is done and a generation later, beside the whole world. Each act reports back with a Chronicle line and a toast, leading with what the power is for.
  • NewThe hand on the map. A map mode rings every act still at work, with its power and the time it has left. Each ring opens the act’s own record.
  • NewLessons of the hand. A new Hand tab in every world lists your acts and gives each power a card: how often and how strongly you used it, what usually came of it by kind of land and strength, and what it was refused for.
  • ChangedWatching changes nothing. The reports are an observer: a world runs the same history with them or without them.
1.5

Inventions That Travel

Chunk 527 October

Knowledge spreads across the map by trade, war and theft, and some of it is never meant to leave home.

  • NewKnowledge fronts. Every realm knows how it came by each invention: research, trade, a neighbour, conquered towns, an arms race, a scholar, refugees, a ruin or the gods. The Chronicle tells an invention’s first arrival in a land or among a people, and a new Knowledge map shades realms by when they learned it.
  • NewTiers of knowledge. Uncommon inventions (bronze, riding, irrigation, sailing, ships, banking, gunpowder, steam, railways, combustion and more) cost three times as much to invent without the land for them, so a realm often waits to learn them from others.
  • NewRare arts. Ten Historical secrets, kept in a workshop town, carried off by conquest, defection, theft or alliance, lost and dug up again.
  • NewMythicA rare art for every people but humans, plus two open to all. Humans alone can learn other peoples’ arts.
  • New25 new turning-point inventions, each working through its own system: knights, cannon and star forts, galleys, banking houses, colonies, telegraph reach. The printing press splits faiths and spreads revolutions. Every invention’s requirements were reviewed to make sense (paper now comes from writing and weaving).
  • NewLibraries and archives. A library keeps its realm’s newest inventions, and a Great Library keeps all of them. They burn when their towns are razed or sacked, carry a realm’s knowledge through a collapse, and leave archives in ruins for later digs.
  • BalanceThe next age’s pull starts one invention sooner, so worlds still reach the Classical Age at about the old pace.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.4:
    PopulationRealmsTownsWars
    Historical+13 %−2 %−2 %−18 %
    Mythic+13 %−4 %−7 %−17 %
  • SpeedThe new code costs about 2 %. The richer, more populous worlds it makes cost more: about 6.7 % slower for Historical worlds and 20 % for Mythic.
1.4

Character

Chunk 426 October

Rulers get a nature, skills, scars and ambitions, and history bends around them.

  • NewTemperament. Rulers, heirs and great people have ambition, mercy, piety, greed, honour and boldness, and their named traits are read from these. Temperament weighs on war, envoys, razing and taxes, and the Chronicle says when a ruler’s nature started a war.
  • NewSkills. War, diplomacy, stewardship, intrigue and learning grow with age and use and fade in old age. A good steward fills the treasury, a diplomat is better liked, a master of intrigue survives plots and a learned ruler speeds research.
  • NewWarrior kings. A ruler skilled at war leads the army in person, more often in early ages and with small hosts. Well guarded, they fall an eighth as often as a captain does.
  • NewBody and health. Rulers and generals take wounds in battle (a lost eye or hand, a limp, a scar, sometimes a by-name to match), can be captured and ransomed, fall ill with a lasting sickness that is named when they die, and may go mad late in life.
  • NewMythicEach race falls ill at its own rate, counted in its own years, so long-lived peoples no longer sicken more often.
  • NewAmbitions. A ruler may want a claimed town, vengeance, to unite their people, to convert a neighbour, a great work or a golden age. Ambitions bend their wars until met or given up, and heirs may swear to finish them. Great generals want glory or power and may lead a coup. Plots stay rare.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.3:
    PopulationRealmsTownsWars
    Historical−6 %+55 %+5 %+32 %
    Mythic+6 %−12 %+4 %+5 %
     Historical worlds break up more and fight more: rulers read from temperament are a little hungrier for war, and it compounds.
1.3

The War Machine

Chunk 226 October

Armies march as companies, soldiers want paying, and battles are fought with a plan.

  • NewArmy companies. Spears, blades, bows (then crossbows, then shot), light and heavy horse, and engineers (later guns). Each realm fields them by its era, land and temper. Battles weigh each company against the enemy’s, and losses fall where the enemy was strong.
  • NewArms and armour. Seven tiers of gear, from stone to modern arms, with quality set by the realm’s mines and workshops. Each tier above the enemy’s is worth a sixth more.
  • NewFour ways to raise an army: a levy, a feudal host, a paid standing army or hired swords, chosen by era, government, wealth and how often the realm fights, and changed by reform.
  • ChangedSoldiers are paid every month, so war drains treasuries. Unpaid armies lose heart and start deserting after two months, and an unpaid standing army may, rarely, overthrow its ruler.
  • NewVeterans and regiments. Standing regiments have names (the First Legion, the Queen’s Lancers), battle honours named for the field, and last stands. Levies come home as veterans who stiffen the next levy.
  • NewBattles in full. Ground and weather bend each arm, and each side fights by a plan: shield wall, pike square, feigned retreat, hammer and anvil, volley fire, night attack. There is a pursuit, prisoners are ransomed at the peace, and the Chronicle tells the decisive moment. A realm copies the plans that beat it.
  • NewMythicEach people fights its own way, with war beasts in the line and its own mounts (wolves, ponies) whether or not it knows Horseback Riding.
  • BalanceTreasuries feel war. The typical realm now holds about 20 years of income instead of 100. A realm at full mobilisation with two years saved runs dry in about four. Battles rose 41 % and towns taken 30 %.
  • BalanceTwelve test worlds at Year 1000, against 1.2.1:
    PopulationRealmsTownsWars
    Historical+3 %−23 %−4 %+4 %
    Mythic−1 %+77 %+13 %+92 %
     Mythic worlds are now far more broken up and war-torn. That is intended: fantasy worlds should be.
  • SpeedHistorical worlds run 5.3 % slower, mostly from more armies in the field. Mythic worlds are unchanged.

Why Wars Start and How They End

Chunk 326 October

Every war is fought for something, every peace has terms, and towns never quite forget.

  • NewClaims on land. A town a realm founded, held for ten years or kept as its seat is claimed when it is lost. Claims fade over generations, pass to an heir realm, drive wars of reconquest and keep relations with the holder cool. Realm and town dossiers list them.
  • NewMythicEach people holds its claims for its own span, and dwarves hold theirs longest.
  • NewWar aims. Each side fights for something named: towns to take or win back, a crown, a faith, tribute, to humble or subjugate the foe, or its own freedom.
  • NewPeace terms written from the winner’s aim: border changes, reparations, an heir held hostage, a crown joined in personal union, a faith imposed, claims renounced. A won holy war converts the loser’s crown. A harsh peace is not forgiven: grievance rises and lost towns stay claimed longer.
  • NewTown memory. A town remembers who sacked it, razed its neighbours, besieged it, saved it, freed it or gave it away, and forgets slowly. It holds out against a realm it hates, may open its gates to one it remembers kindly, and a rising goes over to the realm it remembers best.
  • NewDebt between crowns. Realms at war or short of money borrow from friendly realms and, from the Medieval Age, from banking houses in rich trade towns. Interest follows the borrower’s record. A default costs legitimacy and credit, can ruin a house, and gives a lending realm a cause for war. Reparations can be defaulted on too.
  • BalanceMore wars of reconquest, fewer holy wars. Across the test worlds, wars of reconquest rose from 174 to 254 and holy wars fell from 318 to 211.

    Historical−4 %+3 %+6 %+17 %
    Mythic−3 %+7 %−3 %−14 %

People in Numbers

Chunk 126 October

Towns stop being a single number. Every one now knows its people by age, sex, people and trade.

  • NewAge cohorts. Every town keeps age bands by sex. Births come from its women and their partners, deaths come by age, every loss takes the right people, and manpower comes from the young men.
  • NewMythicEvery people ages by its own life, with its own set of bands. An elf’s first band is its first 25 years.
  • NewBirth and death by people. Each people is born, dies and ages at its own rates, so a town’s mix shifts through real births and deaths. War widows lower births for a while, and widows remarry sooner after great losses.
  • NewMythicCrowded peoples feud. As a crowded town fills with one people’s own kind, it feuds at its own strength, so no people swamps the rest. Elves breed with the magic around them and fade as the weave thins.
  • NewOccupations. Eleven trades in every town, from farmers to scholars and labourers, following its fields, garrison, mine and buildings. The skilled trades take time to grow, and in early eras children of 10 to 14 work at half. Food, production, commerce and research all read the trades.
  • NewMythicFour rare magical trades: mages, healers, artificers and beast-keepers.
  • NewSee the people. The town dossier has an age pyramid stacked by people and a trades bar, and the realm dossier sums them. Three new Statistics charts cover ages, births and deaths, life expectancy and trades. The Chronicle tells the town’s people: widows, baby booms, fields short of hands, and hollow generations as they age.

The Copperplate Atlas

23 to 24 Octoberbefore the Deep Dive

The baseline every number above is measured from.

  • NewThe Copperplate look. An engraved table and neatline, the folio with its gauges and siege dial, the time bridge with its volvelle, letters and engraved icons.
  • NewTitle screen and forging. You watch the world form before it melts into the game. New World is a rite of eight steps over the whole map.
  • NewThe Contents, Options, Every key and the Archive.
